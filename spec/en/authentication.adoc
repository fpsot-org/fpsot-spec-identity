= Sign-in and authenticators
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

These rules set which authenticators a person can use at each assurance level.
They also cover backup authenticators, the recovery of an account and a suspected compromise.
The protocol of the sign-in itself is on the page xref:clients.adoc[Sign-in flows and clients].

== Multifactor authentication

[[IAM-010]]
*IAM-010.* A service at the `substantial` or `high` level MUST require multifactor authentication.
The {term-identity-provider} MUST offer at least one phishing-resistant authenticator to every person, such as a passkey.
A phishing-resistant authenticator proves that it holds a key bound to the real site.
link:https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/[Web Authentication Level 3] works this way.
A code that the person types never resists phishing, whatever the channel.
Phishing-resistant authenticators stop the adversary-in-the-middle attacks that defeat codes.

[[IAM-011]]
*IAM-011.* At the `high` level, one factor MUST be a device-bound key that no one can export, held in hardware.
The identity provider MUST refuse a synced passkey at this level.
With Web Authentication, it MUST check that the backup eligibility flag of the credential is not set.
A synced passkey MAY serve as a phishing-resistant factor at the `low` and `substantial` levels for the public.
A synced key lives in a cloud account, so its safety depends on that account.

The authenticator sets that flag itself, so the flag alone proves nothing.
At the `high` level, the identity provider MUST also verify each new authenticator through its attestation.
It MUST check the attestation against a trusted metadata source, or against a list of keys that the organization issued.
link:https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html[NIST SP 800-63B-4] requires a provider to know the traits of each authenticator it binds, such as through attestation.

[[IAM-012]]
*IAM-012.* Staff MUST sign in with phishing-resistant multifactor authentication.
Administrators of the identity provider, of the {term-authorization-engine} and of their keys MUST use the `high` level.
An organization MAY refuse synced passkeys for its staff at every level.
Staff accounts reach many records at once, and attackers target them first.

[[IAM-013]]
*IAM-013.* The identity provider MUST let each person choose among at least two kinds of authenticator at each level.
It MUST let each person enroll a backup authenticator of the same level, before they need it.
A person who loses the only authenticator would otherwise lose access, or fall back to a weak recovery.
The Québec rules require this choice and these backups.

== Weak authenticators

[[IAM-014]]
*IAM-014.* A password MUST follow the rules on passwords of link:https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html[NIST SP 800-63B-4].
A password MUST have at least 15 characters when it is the only factor, and at least 8 otherwise.
The identity provider MUST accept at least 64 characters and paste from a password manager.
It MUST check each new password against a list of breached and common passwords.
It MUST NOT impose composition rules or a periodic change, and MUST NOT use security questions or hints.
Forced changes and composition rules lead people to weaker, predictable passwords.

The identity provider MUST store each password salted and hashed with a password hashing scheme, under section 3.1.1.2.
It MUST allow at most 100 failed attempts in a row on one account, under section 3.2.2.
Salted hashes slow an attacker who steals the store, and the limit stops guessing online.

[[IAM-015]]
*IAM-015.* A code sent by text message or voice call SHOULD NOT serve as a factor.
It MAY serve as a fallback that the person chooses.
It MUST NOT serve as a factor at the `high` level.
Email MUST NOT serve as an authenticator at any level.
A push approval MUST show a number that the person matches, or MUST NOT serve as a factor.
Attackers take over phone numbers and mailboxes, and people approve a push request out of habit.

== Recovery and compromise

[[IAM-016]]
*IAM-016.* Account recovery MUST NOT give access at a level above the evidence that the recovery checked.
To regain `substantial` or `high`, a person MUST use a backup authenticator of that level.
The person MAY instead pass identity verification again.
Each recovery MUST send a notice to the person through a contact they confirmed earlier.
Attackers use a weak recovery path to bypass a strong sign-in.

[[IAM-017]]
*IAM-017.* A service MUST let a person report a suspected misuse of their account.
It MUST tell the person when it detects one.
On a suspected compromise, the identity provider MUST block the account at once.
It MUST end its sessions and revoke its refresh tokens, under IAM-041.
The service MUST then review the actions of the account since the suspected start, and cancel those it cannot trust.
The Québec rules require such notice, a fast block and the canceling of related actions.

== Sign-in pages

Sign-in, enrollment and recovery pages follow the FPSOT accessibility specification, ACC-001.
That baseline includes the success criterion of link:https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/[WCAG 2.2] on accessible sign-in.
They offer English and French under LOC-003, and pick the language under LOC-005.
Their messages follow WRT-040.
