= Assurance levels
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

Each service needs a known level of trust that a person is who they claim to be.
This page sets the three {abbr-fpsot-first} assurance levels and how a service picks one.
The rest of the specification raises its rules with the level.

== The three levels

[[IAM-001]]
*IAM-001.* Each service MUST declare the {abbr-fpsot} assurance level that it requires: `low`, `substantial` or `high`.
It MUST choose the level from a documented assessment, which weighs the harm that a wrong identity could cause.
The assessment MUST cover the confidentiality, the integrity and the availability of the information.
A service that needs no sign-in MUST still record the assessment and its result.
A public body in Québec must assess every service in the same way, so one assessment serves both purposes.
The table gives the minimum of each level.

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Level |Identity verification |Authentication
|`low` |The person states their own details, or the service asks for none. |One factor, under IAM-014.
|`substantial` |The service checks evidence of identity, such as a photo identity document, against a trusted source. |Multifactor authentication, with a phishing-resistant option on offer, under IAM-010. Synced passkeys count.
|`high` |An agent or an equivalent means checks several pieces of evidence, and corroborates them with a trusted source. |Phishing-resistant multifactor authentication with a device-bound key in hardware, under IAM-011.
|===

[[IAM-002]]
*IAM-002.* The `acr` claim of an identity token or an access token MUST hold one of these values.

* `https://fpsot.org/assurance-levels/low`
* `https://fpsot.org/assurance-levels/substantial`
* `https://fpsot.org/assurance-levels/high`

The `acr` value MUST name the level that the current sign-in meets, not the method.
The methods go in the `amr` claim, with the values of the link:https://www.iana.org/assignments/jwt/jwt.xhtml[JSON Web Token claims registry] and link:https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc8176[RFC 8176].
A level URI keeps its meaning across products and countries, while a method name says nothing about strength.
The URIs follow NAM-030 and NAM-083, so they stay stable.

[[IAM-003]]
*IAM-003.* A service MUST pick an {abbr-fpsot} level at least as strict as the level that its jurisdiction requires.
It MUST read the mapping in the file `registry/assurance-levels.yaml` of this repository.
The service MUST confirm with the authority each mapping that carries the mark _to be verified_.
A jurisdiction can set a stricter detail, such as a ban on codes sent by text message.
The service MUST also meet that detail.

The table below repeats the mapping for readers.

[cols="1,2,2,1,1,2",options="header"]
|===
|{abbr-fpsot} level |Québec |Canada |Pan-Canadian Trust Framework |eIDAS |{abbr-nist-first} SP 800-63-4
|`low` |ID1 and AU1 |Credential level 2, to be verified |Level 1 or 2, to be verified |Low, to be verified |IAL1 and AAL1
|`substantial` |ID2 and AU2, or ID3 and AU3 |Credential level 3 |Level 3, to be verified |Substantial, to be verified |IAL2 and AAL2
|`high` |ID4 and AU4 |Credential level 4 |Level 4, to be verified |High, to be verified |IAL3 and AAL3, with FAL2 or more
|===

The Québec column comes from the link:https://www.publicationsduquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fileadmin/gazette/pdf_encrypte/lois_reglements/2024F/83715.pdf[Québec rules on digital identity assurance].
It also follows the Québec instruction IA-SI-2022-001-OP.
The Canada column comes from the federal link:https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/guideline-multi-factor-authentication.html[guideline on multifactor authentication].
The other columns come from the link:https://diacc.ca/trust-framework/[Pan-Canadian Trust Framework], the link:https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2015/1502/oj[eIDAS levels regulation] and link:https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/63/4/final[NIST SP 800-63-4].

[[IAM-004]]
*IAM-004.* An identity provider MUST NOT state an `acr` level above the identity verification that the account has passed.
A strong authenticator on an unverified account proves only that the same person came back.
A service SHOULD record the identity verification level and the authentication level of an account as two separate facts.
It can then raise one without the other, as the Québec rules and {abbr-nist} SP 800-63-4 do.

== Digital wallets

This version sets no rule on digital identity wallets or verifiable credentials.
European wallets and link:https://openid.net/specs/openid-4-verifiable-presentations-1_0-final.html[OpenID for Verifiable Presentations 1.0] may lead to rules later.
So may the planned Québec wallet.
Until then, a wallet can serve only as one more way to verify identity under IAM-001.
