= Representation and delegation
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

People often act for someone else, such as a parent for a child or an employee for a business.
These rules set how a service knows who acts, for whom, and within which limits.
In Québec, articles 13 to 16 of the link:https://www.publicationsduquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fileadmin/gazette/pdf_encrypte/lois_reglements/2024F/83715.pdf[rules on digital identity] require a proof of capacity here.

No shared Canadian service supports representation yet.
A link:https://www.biometricupdate.com/202610/canada-begins-gradual-rollout-of-sso-for-digital-government-services[news report], which is a secondary source, covers the federal sign-in service, CanadaLogin.
It says that the service plans delegation only after its current funding ends, in early 2028.
This page fills that gap with a model that any service can adopt now.

== People act as themselves

[[IAM-060]]
*IAM-060.* Each person MUST sign in with their own account, and an account MUST belong to one person only.
A service MUST NOT offer a shared account, even for a business or a team.
A business or a public body acts only through the people who represent it.
The Québec rules state the same principle of one account per person.
A shared account hides who did what, and the audit trail loses its value.

== Relationships of representation

[[IAM-061]]
*IAM-061.* Each representation MUST be a relationship in the {term-authorization-engine}, from the representative to the represented party.
It MUST state its kind, such as guardian or mandatary, with its scope, its start and its end.
It MUST hold a reference to the proof of capacity, such as a court decision or a mandate.
For a business, the proof MAY be an entry in the Québec enterprise register.
It MUST also record who checked that proof, and when.
A token claim cannot follow a scope or an end date that changes after the sign-in.

[[IAM-062]]
*IAM-062.* The represented party MUST be a `Party` under DMO-040, a person or an organization.
The service SHOULD keep each representation as a `PartyRelationship` under DMO-044, with its period.
It SHOULD derive the relationships of the engine from those records.
One record of the business fact then feeds both the case files and the access decisions.

[[IAM-063]]
*IAM-063.* The service checks the capacity of the representative of a business.
That representative MUST be able to grant other people access to the account of the business, and to revoke it.
A grant MUST NOT exceed the scope of the person who gives it.
Each grant MUST be its own relationship under IAM-061, with a reference to the representative who made it.
The Québec rules make that representative responsible for the access to the account of the business.

[[IAM-064]]
*IAM-064.* A person who represents someone else MUST choose, in the session, the party they act for.
The service MUST show that choice on every page.
Each request MUST pass the represented party to the engine, in the subject properties or the context of the evaluation.
The engine MUST deny when no valid relationship covers the action.
A visible choice prevents a person from acting for the wrong child or the wrong business by mistake.

== Token exchange

[[IAM-065]]
*IAM-065.* Within one organization, a service MAY exchange the token of a person for a new token.
The new token MUST follow link:https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc8693[RFC 8693], with a narrower audience and a subset of the scopes.
Its `sub` claim MUST name the person, and its `act` claim MUST name the service that acts.
A token obtained by token exchange MUST carry that `act` claim, so that it never impersonates the person.
A token exchange that crosses from one organization to another MUST NOT happen in this version.
Whether user context may cross between public bodies is a legal question, listed on the page xref:questions-for-counsel.adoc[Questions for counsel].
